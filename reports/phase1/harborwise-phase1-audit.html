Can we predict when a berth will be free?

An audit of the 2019 Caribbean AIS port-call data. It checks what each record means, whether berth visits can be rebuilt from it, and what the models can safely train on.

Record semantics

What one port call means

A port call starts when a ship's speed over ground drops below 0.5 knots and ends when it rises back above it. Only AIS messages within about 600 m of a surveyed quay or wharf count. It is a stop alongside a quay, recorded at port level. It is not an anchorage stop, and it does not name the berth. We recover the berth by testing the stop position against berth polygons. Joined that way, ts_start and ts_end work as berth entry and exit.

Timezone. The paper doesn't state one. Arrivals reach their minimum at 06–08 UTC (01–03 local time in the Caribbean) and peak between 12 and 23 UTC. That fits UTC timestamps.

Geometry

Berth matching on the full table

  • Port calls, berths and ports all use EPSG:4326. Point geometries agree with the lat/lon columns to within 0.000007°.
  • 82.67% of calls with coordinates at the 251 ports that have berth polygons fall inside a polygon. This agrees with the earlier sample estimate of 81.63%.
  • Our per-berth call counts correlate at 0.994 with the published berths.number, with a median ratio of exactly 1.00. We reproduced the dataset authors' own berth assignment.
  • 78,753 unmatched points lie within 50 m of a polygon, and 150,018 within 200 m. A small buffer would recover many of them. That is BE2's decision, and no buffer is applied yet.
  • Polygon sizes range from about 760 m² (10th percentile) to 1.6 km² (99th percentile). The largest "berths" are whole basins.
Scope decision

Who generates the calls

Most calls come from small craft that stop and start constantly around the harbour. HarborWise is about large ships waiting for a berth, so the models should train on commercial types only.

Calls → visits

Merging split stays

A ship that shifts slightly at its berth can produce several calls. Consecutive calls by the same ship at the same berth are separated by a median gap of 1.19 h. Merging at 0.5 h changes nothing, which shows the authors already merge gaps under about 30 minutes. For commercial ships the result barely moves beyond 1 h, so the label holds up whichever threshold we pick.

For commercial ships, "the berth is free" is a clean yes/no for most berths. The 138 berths shared more than 10% of the time need a capacity of 2 or more, with clearance defined as "one slot frees".

Training set

The commercial berth-visit table

  • 51,747 visits by 6,218 ships at 832 berths in 159 ports
  • 263 berths have at least 50 visits and 131 have at least 100. Those 131 berths hold 63% of all visits.
  • Stay length: 10th percentile 7.6 h, median 23.1 h, 90th percentile 72.8 h, 99th percentile 204 h
  • Median stay by type: bulk 54 h, tanker 34 h, general cargo 26 h, container 15 h, cruise 10 h
  • 3,400–4,900 visits a month, steady through the year. The Jan–Aug / Sep–Oct / Nov–Dec chronological split has enough data in each part.

What this dataset cannot do

  • Measure anchorage waiting. Calls cover stops near quays only. trajectories.csv has one row per segment between stops with no positions, and areapresence.csv only gives daily sea-zone codes. The simulation must derive the wait from predicted clearance and say so on the data-honesty slide.
  • Give distance or speed on approach. There are no positions in transit. The optimizer takes distance from the user, or a rough route length from segments_port2port.
  • Describe cargo operations. There is no crane, tonnage or cargo data. Features are limited to port, berth, vessel type, timing and congestion.
Handoffs

What each person should do with this

ALL

The label is a commercial berth visit: arrival when speed falls below 0.5 kn inside the berth polygon, departure when it rises above, with gaps under 1 h merged. All times are UTC.

BE2

Build checkpoints from berth_visits_merged_1h.parquet, keeping is_commercial and confidence_min ≥ 0.5. Drop year-censored visits. Test with and without stays over 7 days. Key on berth_key. Try harbour busyness (all-vessel calls in the past 24 h) as a feature.

BE3

With a median stay of 23 h, "clears within 3 h" will be rare early in a visit, so the imbalance experiments are justified. Choose N once BE2's checkpoint distribution exists.

BE4

Model the anchorage wait rather than reading it from history. Give the 138 shared berths a capacity of 2 or more.

FE2

Use the funnel above on the data-honesty slide: 1.49 M calls → 915 k at a berth → 782 k visits → 51.7 k commercial visits, with the reason for each drop.
